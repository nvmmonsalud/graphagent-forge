# Daytona HackSprint Tokyo 2026

*Source document for the seed corpus. Fixture `source_doc`: `Daytona HackSprint Tokyo 2026`.*

## The event

The Daytona HackSprint Tokyo 2026 is a 48-hour hackathon held in Tokyo and organised
around sandboxed developer environments. Teams ship a working demo and defend it live
in front of a judging panel on Demo Day. **Daytona**, the developer-environment company,
is the title sponsor: it runs the event, funds the grand prize, and staffs mentor office
hours for participating teams.

## Venue

The event is held at **Shibuya QWS**, a co-working and event venue located in the Shibuya
ward of Tokyo. One main hall is given over to hacking; a smaller studio is used for Demo
Day pitches.

## Schedule

The HackSprint opens with the **48-Hour Sprint Window**, a continuous build period. Code
written before the window opens is disallowed — everything demoed must be built inside it.
**Mentor Office Hours** run throughout the sprint in scheduled 20-minute slots, where teams
bring architecture and integration questions to visiting engineers. Daytona engineers staff
those slots.

Submissions freeze when the sprint window closes, two hours before **Demo Day**, the closing
session. Each team gets six minutes on stage plus three minutes of questions from the judging
panel, and the grand prize is awarded at the end.

## Judging

Every Demo Day pitch is scored by the **Judging Panel**, a three-person panel that applies the
published rubric and selects the grand prize winner.

- **Aiko Tanaka** — head judge, a distinguished engineer working on graph databases. She chairs
  the panel and owns the technical depth score.
- **Marcus Weill** — judge and developer-relations lead. He weighs how deeply each project
  actually uses the partner services rather than merely name-checking them, and owns the
  partner-integration score.
- **Priya Raghunathan** — judge and product designer. She scores the live demo: whether the
  thing on screen works, recovers from failure, and tells a coherent story.

The **Judging Rubric** is the published scoring sheet, with three weighted criteria:

| Criterion | Weight | Rewards |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Technical Depth Criterion** | 40% | Non-trivial system design: real data flow, real failure handling, and code the team can explain line by line. |
| **Sponsor Integration Criterion** | 35% | Genuine use of a partner service, with credit for graceful behaviour when the partner API key is absent. |
| **Live Demo Criterion** | 25% | A demo that runs live on stage without a video fallback and survives an unrehearsed question. |

## Prize and entrants

The **Grand Prize** is the top HackSprint award — a cash prize plus a year of credits on the
partner platforms — funded by Daytona and handed out at the close of Demo Day.

**Team GraphAgent Forge** is a four-person team entering the HackSprint with a knowledge-graph
agent that ingests documents and answers questions over the resulting graph. The team booked two
office-hour slots during the sprint, filed its repository URL and written pitch through the
**Submission Portal**, and pitches on Demo Day. The panel reads each entry out of the submission
portal before judging begins.
